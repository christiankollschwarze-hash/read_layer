ACT I.

SCENE I. _On a ship at sea: a stormy noise of thunder and lightning heard._

_Enter _a Ship-Master_ and _a Boatswain_._

_Mast._ Boatswain!

_Boats._ Here, master: what’s the news?

_Mast._ Good, speak to the sailors. Get to work quickly, or we’ll run ourselves aground. Hurry, hurry.    [_Exit._

_Enter _Mariners_._

_Boats._ Hey, my hearts! Cheerfully, cheerfully, my hearts! Quickly, quickly! Take in the topsail. Pay attention to the master’s whistle. Blow, until you burst, if there’s enough room!

###CHAPTER### _enter Alonso, Sebastian, Antonio, Ferdinand, Gonzalo,

A ship at sea. A storm with thunder and lightning. Enter a Shipmaster and a Boatswain.

Alonso. Good boatswain, be careful. Where is the master? Get the men to act bravely.

Boatswain. Please, go below now.

Antonio. Where is the master, boatswain?

Boatswain. Can you not hear him? You are getting in the way of our work. Stay in your cabins. You are helping the storm.

Gonzalo. Come now, good man, be patient.

Boatswain. I will be patient when the sea is calm. Get away from here! What do these roaring waves care for the name of king? Go to your cabin. Be quiet! Do not trouble us.

Gonzalo. Good man, remember who you have on board.

Boatswain. No one that I love more than myself. You are a councillor. If you can command these elements to be silent and bring peace to this present moment, we will not handle a rope again. Use your authority. If you cannot, be thankful you have lived so long, and get yourself ready in your cabin for the misfortune of this hour, if it should happen. Cheerfully, good men! Out of our way, I say. [Exit.]

Gonzalo. I take great comfort from this fellow. I think he has no sign of drowning about him; his face looks perfectly like a man born to be hanged. Stand firm, good Fate, to his hanging. Make the rope of his destiny our anchor cable, for our own cable does us little good. If he is not born to be hanged, our situation is miserable. [Exeunt.]

Re-enter Boatswain.

Boatswain. Down with the topmast! Quickly! Lower, lower! Bring her to lie close to the wind with the mainsail. [A cry within.] A plague upon this howling! They are louder than the weather or our work.

Re-enter Sebastian, Antonio, and Gonzalo.

Yet again! What are you doing here? Should we give up and drown? Do you want to sink?

Sebastian. A pox on your throat, you bawling, blasphemous, uncharitable dog!

Boatswain. Then work, you.

Antonio. Hang, you cur! Hang, you whoreson, insolent noisemaker. We are less afraid of drowning than you are.

Gonzalo. I will guarantee he will not drown, even if the ship were no stronger than a nutshell and as leaky as an unstopped woman.

Boatswain. Lay her close to the wind, close to the wind! Set her two sails off to sea again; turn her away from the shore.

Enter Mariners wet.

Mariners. All is lost! To prayers, to prayers! All is lost!

Boatswain. What, must our mouths be cold?

Gonzalo. The king and prince are at prayers! Let us join them, for our situation is the same as theirs.

Sebastian. I am out of patience.

Antonio. We are simply cheated of our lives by drunkards. This wide-mouthed rascal—I wish you might lie drowning through the washing of ten tides!

Gonzalo. He will still be hanged,

Even if every drop of water swears against it and opens its mouth widest to swallow him.

[A confused noise within: “Mercy on us!”— “We split, we split!”— “Farewell my wife and children!”— “Farewell, brother!”— “We split, we split, we split!”]

Antonio. Let us all sink with the king.

Sebastian. Let us take leave of him. [Exeunt Antonio and Sebastian.]

Gonzalo. Now I would give a thousand furlongs of sea for an acre of barren ground—long heath, brown furze, anything. The wills above be done! But I would gladly die a dry death. [Exeunt.]

SCENE II. _The island. Before PROSPERO’S cell._

_Enter PROSPERO and MIRANDA._

_Mir._ If by your art, my dearest father, you have Put the wild waters in this roar, allay them.

The sky, it seems, would pour down stinking pitch, But that the sea, mounting to the welkin’s cheek, Dashes the fire out. O, I have suffer’d                              5 With those that I saw suffer! a brave vessel,

Who had, no doubt, some noble creature in her, Dash’d all to pieces. O, the cry did knock

Against my very heart! Poor souls, they perish’d! Had I been any god of power, I would                                10 Have sunk the sea within the earth, or ere

It should the good ship so have swallow’d and

The fraughting souls within her.

_Pros._                        Be collected:

No more amazement: tell your piteous heart

There’s no harm done.

_Mir._              O, woe the day!

_Pros._                           No harm.                          15 I have done nothing but in care of thee,

Of thee, my dear one, thee, my daughter, who

I have with such provision in mine art

So safely order’d, that there is no soul,

Which thou heard’st cry, which thou saw’st sink. Sit down; For thou must now know farther.

_Mir._                        You have often

_Pros._                The hour’s now come;

The very minute bids thee ope thine ear;

A time before we came unto this cell?

_Mir._ Certainly, sir, I can.

_Pros._ By what? By any other house or person? Tell me anything that

_Mir._ It is far off,

and more like a dream than something my memory can confirm as real. Did I not once have

four or five women who took care of me?

_Pros._ You did, and more, Miranda. But how is it that this still lives in your mind? What else do you see in the dark backward and abyss of time? If you remember anything before you came here, you may also remember how you came here.

_Mir._ But that I do not.

_Pros._ Twelve years ago, Miranda, twelve years ago, your father was the Duke of Milan, and

a prince of power.

_Mir._ Sir, are you not my father?

_Pros._ Your mother was a model of virtue, and

she said you were my daughter; and your father was Duke of Milan; and his only heir

and princess, no less nobly born.

_Mir._ O heavens!

What foul play happened to us, that we came from there? Or was it blessed that we did?

_Pros._ Both, both, my girl.

By foul play, as you say, we were driven from there; but blessedly helped to get here.

_Mir._ O, my heart bleeds

to think of the trouble I have caused you, which is gone from my memory! Please, go on.

_Pros._ My brother, and your uncle, called Antonio—I beg you, listen to me—that a brother should

be so treacherous!—he whom, next to yourself,

of all the world I loved, and to him I gave

the management of my state; for at that time, among all the lordships it was the first,

and Prospero the foremost duke, being so regarded

in dignity, and for the liberal arts

without equal; those being all my study,

I cast the government upon my brother, and grew stranger to my state, being carried away and absorbed in secret studies. Your false uncle—

Are you listening to me?

_Mir._ Sir, most attentively.

_Pros._ Once he had perfected how to grant requests, how to deny them, whom to promote, and whom to cut down for rising too high, he newly created

the creatures that were mine, I say, or changed them, or else newly formed them; having both the key

of officer and office, he set all hearts in the state to whatever tune pleased his ear; so that now he was the ivy which had hidden my princely trunk,

and sucked the life out of it. You are not listening.

_Mir._ O, good sir, I am.

_Pros._ I beg you, listen to me.

I, thus neglecting worldly matters, all dedicated to solitude and the improving of my mind

with that which, but for being so withdrawn, was valued above all popular estimation, in my false brother awakened an evil nature; and my trust,

like a good parent, produced in him

a falsehood in its opposite, as great

as my trust was; which indeed had no limit, a confidence without bound. He being thus made lord, not only with what my revenue yielded,

but what my power might also demand, like one

who, by telling a lie so often, had made

such a sinner of his memory as to credit his own lie, he did believe

he was indeed the duke; out of the substitution, and performing the outward face of royalty,

with all its privileges:—hence his ambition growing.—Do you hear?

_Mir._ Your tale, sir, would cure deafness.

_Pros._ To have no barrier between the part he played and the man he played it for, he must become

Absolute ruler of Milan. Me, poor man, my library

Was kingdom enough for me: of worldly power he now thinks me incapable; he conspires,

So hungry was he for control, with the King of Naples to pay him annual tribute, do him homage,

Subject his crown to the King's crown, and bend

The dukedom, still unbowed—alas, poor Milan!—to most shameful submission.

_Mir._ O heavens!

_Pros._ Note his terms and what followed; then tell me if this could be a brother.

_Mir._ I would be wronging

My grandmother to think anything but nobly of her:

Good mothers have borne bad sons.

_Pros._ Now the terms. This King of Naples, being an old

And bitter enemy of mine, listens to my brother's request;

Which was that he, in return for the promises

Of homage and I know not how much tribute,

Should immediately remove me and mine

From the dukedom, and give fair Milan,

With all its honors, to my brother: upon which,

A treacherous army was raised, and one midnight

Chosen for the purpose, Antonio opened

The gates of Milan; and, in the dead of darkness, the agents for the purpose hurried out

Me and your crying self.

_Mir._ Alas, how pitiful!

I, not remembering how I cried out then,

Will cry it over again: it is a memory

That forces tears from my eyes.

_Pros._ Hear a little more, and then I'll bring you to the present business

That now concerns us; without which, this story would be quite irrelevant.

_Mir._ Why did they not

Destroy us that very hour?

_Pros._ Well asked, my girl:

My tale prompts that question. Dear, they dared not,

So deep was the love my people bore me; nor set

A mark so bloody on the business; but

With fairer colors painted their foul purposes.

In short, they hurried us aboard a ship,

Carried us some leagues out to sea; where they prepared

A rotten carcass of a boat, not rigged,

Nor with tackle, sail, nor mast; the very rats

Had instinctively abandoned it: there they put us,

To cry to the sea that roared at us; to sigh

To the winds, whose pity, sighing back again,

Did us only loving harm.

_Mir._ Alas, what trouble

I must have been to you then!

_Pros._ O, you were a cherub

That preserved me. You smiled,

Infused with a strength from heaven,

When I had strewn the sea with drops of salt tears,

Groaning under my burden; which raised in me

An enduring spirit, to bear up

Against whatever should follow.

_Mir._ How did we come ashore?

_Pros._ By divine Providence.

We had some food and some fresh water, which

Have I, your schoolmaster, made you more accomplished than other princesses can be, who have more time for idle pleasures and less careful tutors?

_Mir._ Heaven thank you for it! And now, please, sir—for it is still beating in my mind—tell me your reason for raising this sea-storm.

_Pros._ Know this much. By a most strange accident, generous Fortune—

now my dear lady—has brought my enemies

to this shore; and by my foresight I see my fortunes depend upon

a most favorable star, whose influence,

if I do not seize now but instead neglect, my fortunes

will forever after decline. Stop asking questions now. You are inclined to sleep; it is a good drowsiness, and let it come. I know you cannot resist. [_Miranda sleeps._] Come away, servant, come. I am ready now.

Approach, my Ariel, come.

_Enter ARIEL._

_Ari._ All hail, great master! Noble sir, hail! I come to answer your every pleasure; whether it is to fly, to swim, to dive into the fire, to ride

on the curled clouds—command Ariel and all his abilities to any task.

_Pros._ Have you, spirit,

carried out the tempest exactly as I ordered?

_Ari._ To every detail. I boarded the king's ship; now at the prow,

now in the middle, on the deck, in every cabin,

I spread terror with flames. Sometimes I would split apart

and burn in many places; on the topmast,

the yards and bowsprit I would flame separately, then meet and join together. Jove's lightnings, the forerunners of the dreadful thunderclaps, were not more instantaneous

and faster than sight. The fire and cracks of sulfurous roaring seemed to besiege the most mighty Neptune

and make his bold waves tremble, yes, make his dread trident shake.

_Pros._ My brave spirit!

Who was so firm, so steady, that this turmoil

would not disturb his reason?

_Ari._ Not a soul

but felt a fever of madness and performed

some acts of desperation. All but the sailors plunged into the foaming brine and abandoned the vessel, which was then all on fire with me. The king's son, Ferdinand, with hair standing on end—then like reeds, not hair—was the first man to leap. He cried, "Hell is empty, and all the devils are here."

_Pros._ Why, that's my spirit! But was this not near the shore?

_Ari._ Close by, my master.

_Pros._ But are they safe, Ariel?

_Ari._ Not a hair was harmed. On their clothes that kept them afloat there was not a stain,

but they were fresher than before. And, as you commanded me, I have scattered them in groups about the island. The king's son I have landed by himself;

I left him cooling the air with sighs

in a secluded corner of the island, and sitting

with his arms folded in this sad knot.

_Pros._ Tell me how you have disposed of the king's ship,

the sailors, and all the rest of the fleet.

_Ari._ The king's ship is safely in harbor, in the deep cove where once you called me up at midnight to fetch dew

from the ever-stormy Bermudas. There she is hidden. The sailors are all stowed below hatches; with a spell added to their exhausted labor,

I have left them asleep. And as for the rest of the fleet, which I scattered, they have all met again and are upon the Mediterranean, afloat.

Bound sadly home for Naples, supposing that they saw the king’s ship wrecked, and his great person perish.

_Pros._ Ariel, thy charge

Exactly is performed: but there’s more work.

What is the time o’ the day?

_Ari._ Past the mid season.

_Pros._ At least two glasses. The time ’twixt six and now must by us both be spent most preciously.

_Ari._ Is there more toil? Since thou dost give me pains, let me remember thee what thou hast promised,

Which is not yet performed me.

_Pros._ How now? moody?

What is’t thou canst demand?

_Ari._ My liberty.

_Pros._ Before the time be out? no more!

_Ari._ I prithee, remember I have done thee worthy service;

Told thee no lies, made thee no mistakings, served without or grudge or grumblings: thou didst promise to bate me a full year.

_Pros._ Dost thou forget from what a torment I did free thee?

_Ari._ No.

_Pros._ Thou dost; and think’st it much to tread the ooze of the salt deep,

To run upon the sharp wind of the north,

To do me business in the veins o’ the earth when it is baked with frost.

_Ari._ I do not, sir.

_Pros._ Thou liest, malignant thing! Hast thou forgot the foul witch Sycorax, who with age and envy

Was grown into a hoop? hast thou forgot her?

_Ari._ No, sir.

_Pros._ Thou hast. Where was she born? speak; tell me.

_Ari._ Sir, in Argier.

_Pros._ O, was she so? I must

Once in a month recount what thou hast been,

Which thou forget’st. This damned witch Sycorax, for mischiefs manifold, and sorceries terrible to enter human hearing, from Argier, thou know’st, was banished: for one thing she did they would not take her life. Is not this true?

_Ari._ Ay, sir.

_Pros._ This blue-eyed hag was hither brought with child, and here was left by the sailors. Thou, my slave, as thou report’st thyself, wast then her servant; and, for thou wast a spirit too delicate

To act her earthy and abhorred commands,

Refusing her grand hests, she did confine thee, by help of her more potent ministers, and in her most unmitigable rage,

Into a cloven pine; within which rift

Imprisoned thou didst painfully remain

A dozen years; within which space she died,

And left thee there; where thou didst vent thy groans as fast as mill-wheels strike. Then was this island—save for the son that she did litter here,

A freckled whelp hag-born—not honoured with

A human shape.

_Ari._ Yes, Caliban her son.

_Pros._ Dull thing, I say so; he, that Caliban, whom now I keep in service. Thou best know’st

What torment I did find thee in; thy groans

Did make wolves howl, and penetrate the breasts of ever-angry bears: it was a torment

To lay upon the damned, which Sycorax could not again undo: it was mine art,

When I arrived and heard thee, that made gape

The pine, and let thee out.

_Ari._ I thank thee, master.

_Pros._ If thou more murmur’st, I will rend an oak, and peg thee in his knotty entrails, till thou hast howled away twelve winters.

_Ari._ Pardon, master: I will be correspondent to command,

And be gentle as you carry out my orders.

_Pros._ Do that, and after two days I will set you free.

_Ari._ That's my noble master!

What shall I do? Tell me; what shall I do?

_Pros._ Go make yourself look like a sea-nymph: be visible to no one but you and me; invisible to every other eye. Go take this shape,

And come here in it: go, hurry now!

[_Exit Ariel._

Wake up, dear heart, wake up! You have slept well; wake up!

_Mir._ The strangeness of your story put

Heaviness in me.

_Pros._ Shake it off. Come on;

We'll visit Caliban my slave, who never

Gives us a kind answer.

_Mir._ He's a villain, sir,

I do not like to look at.

_Pros._ But, as things stand, we cannot do without him: he makes our fire,

Fetches in our wood, and does chores

That benefit us. What, ho! Slave! Caliban!

You lump of earth, you! Speak.

_Cal._ [_within_] There's wood enough inside.

_Pros._ Come out, I say! There's other work for you: come, you tortoise! When?

_Re-enter ARIEL like a water-nymph._

Fine apparition! My clever Ariel,

Listen closely.

_Ari._ My lord, it shall be done. [_Exit._

_Pros._ You poisonous slave, fathered by the devil himself upon your wicked mother, come out!

_Enter CALIBAN._

_Cal._ May as wicked a dew as ever my mother brushed with a raven's feather from an unhealthy marsh

Fall on you both! May a south-west wind blow on you

And blister you all over!

_Pros._ For this, be sure, tonight you shall have cramps, side-stitches that shall stop your breath; goblins shall, for all the long night that they may work, all torment you; you shall be pinched

As thick as honeycomb, each pinch more stinging than the bees that made them.

_Cal._ I must eat my dinner. This island is mine, by Sycorax my mother,

Which you take from me. When you first came, you stroked me and made much of me; you would give me water with berries in it; and teach me how

To name the bigger light, and how the lesser, that burn by day and night: and then I loved you, and showed you all the features of the isle, the fresh springs, brine-pits, barren places and fertile: cursed be I for doing so! May all the charms

Of Sycorax, toads, beetles, bats, fall on you! For I am all the subjects that you have,

Who first was my own king: and here you pen me in this hard rock, while you keep from me

The rest of the island.

_Pros._ You most lying slave,

Whom whipping may move, not kindness! I have treated you, filth as you are, with human care; and lodged you in my own cell, until you tried to violate the honor of my child.

_Cal._ O ho, O ho! Would it had been done!

You prevented me; otherwise I would have peopled this isle with Calibans.

_Pros._ Abhorred slave,

Who will not take on any trace of goodness,

Being capable of all ill! I pitied you,

Took pains to make you speak, taught you each hour one thing or another: when you did not, savage, know your own meaning, but would gabble like a most brutish thing, I gave your intentions

Words that made them known. But your vile nature, though you did learn, had that in it which good natures could not bear to be near; therefore you were deservedly confined into this rock,

You who deserved worse than a prison.

_Cal._ You taught me language; and my profit from it is, I know how to curse. May the red plague rid me of you for teaching me your language!

_Pros._ Hag-seed, get out of here! Fetch us in fuel; and be quick, you'd best,

To answer other business. Do you shrug, you malice? If you neglect, or do unwillingly

What I command, I'll rack you with old cramps, fill all your bones with aches, make you roar so that beasts shall tremble at your din.

_Cal._ No, I pray you. [_Aside_] I must obey: his art is of such power, it would control my mother's god, Setebos,

And make a servant of him.

_Pros._ So, slave; get out of here! [_Exit Caliban._

_ARIEL'S song._

Come unto these yellow sands, and then take hands:

When you have curtsied and kissed

The wild waves into quiet:

Dance nimbly here and there;

And, sweet spirits, bear the burden.

_Burden_ [_dispersedly_]. Hark, hark!

Bow-wow.

The watch-dogs bark:

Bow-wow.

_Ari._ Hark, hark! I hear

The strain of strutting chanticleer cry, Cock-a-diddle-dow.

_Fer._ Where should this music be? In the air or the earth? It sounds no more: and, surely, it waits upon

Some god of this island. Sitting on a bank,

Weeping again over the king my father's wreck, this music crept by me upon the waters,

Calming both their fury and my grief

With its sweet air: from there I have followed it. Or rather it has drawn me. But it is gone.

No, it begins again.

_ARIEL sings._

Full five fathoms deep your father lies;

Of his bones are coral made;

Those are pearls that were his eyes:

Nothing of him that does fade,

But does suffer a sea-change into something rich and strange.

Sea-nymphs hourly ring his funeral bell:

_Burden:_ Ding-dong.

_Ari._ Hark! Now I hear them,--Ding-dong, bell.

_Fer._ The song remembers my drowned father. This is no mortal business, nor any sound

That the earth owns:--I hear it now above me.

_Pros._ Lift the fringed curtains of your eyes, and say what you see over there.

_Mir._ What is it? A spirit? Lord, how it looks about! Believe me, sir, it carries a fine form. But it is a spirit.

_Pros._ No, girl; it eats and sleeps and has such senses as we have, the same. This fine young man whom you see was in the wreck; and, except that he is somewhat stained with grief, which is beauty's canker, you might call him a goodly person: he has lost his companions,

And wanders about to find them.

_Mir._ I might call him

A thing divine; for nothing natural

I ever saw so noble.

_Pros._ [_Aside_] It goes on, I see,

As my soul prompts it. Spirit, fine spirit! I'll free you within two days for this.

_Fer._ Most surely, the goddess on whom these airs attend! Grant my prayer, that I may know if you remain upon this island;

And that you will give me some good instruction

How I may conduct myself here: my chief request, which I do last pronounce, is, O you wonder!

Are you a maiden or not?

_Mir._ No wonder, sir;

But certainly a maiden.

_Fer._ My language! Heavens!

I am the best of those who speak this speech,

If only I were where it is spoken.

_Pros._ What? The best? What would you be if the King of Naples heard you?

_Fer._ A single man, as I am now, who is amazed to hear you speak of Naples. He does hear me; and that he does makes me weep. I myself am Naples,

Who with my own eyes, never dry since then, saw the king my father shipwrecked.

_Mir._ Alas, for mercy!

_Fer._ Yes, truly, and all his lords too; the Duke of Milan and his brave son being two of them.

_Pros._ [_Aside_] The Duke of Milan and his even braver daughter could refute you, if it were fitting to do so now. At first sight they have fallen in love. Delicate Ariel,

I’ll set you free for this. [_To Fer._] A word, good sir; I fear you have done yourself some harm: a word.

_Mir._ Why does my father speak so harshly? This

Is the third man I have ever seen; the first that I have ever sighed for. May pity move my father

To be favorable to my wishes!

_Fer._ O, if you are a maiden,

And your affection has not been given away, I’ll make you the queen of Naples.

_Pros._ Wait, sir! One word more.

[_Aside_] They are both in each other’s power:

but I must make this swift business difficult, lest too easy a winning

Make the prize seem worthless. [_To Fer._] One word more; I command you to listen to me: you here take for yourself

The name you do not own; and have placed yourself

Upon this island as a spy, to win it from me, its lord.

_Fer._ No, as I am a man.

_Mir._ Nothing evil can live in such a temple: if an evil spirit had so fair a house,

Good things would strive to live with it.

_Pros._ Follow me. Do not speak for him; he is a traitor. Come; I’ll chain your neck and feet together:

You shall drink sea-water; your food shall be

Fresh-water mussels, withered roots, and husks in which the acorn was cradled. Follow.

_Fer._ No;

I will resist such treatment until my enemy has more power.

[_Draws, and is charmed from moving._

_Mir._ O dear father,

Do not make too rash a test of him, for

He is gentle, and not frightening.

_Pros._ What! I say,

Should my foot teach me? Put your sword up, traitor;

You make a show, but dare not strike, your conscience is so possessed with guilt: come out of your fighting stance; for I can disarm you here with this stick

And make your weapon drop.

_Mir._ I beg you, father.

_Pros._ Away! Do not hang on my clothes.

_Mir._ Sir, have pity; I’ll be his guarantee.

_Pros._ Silence! One more word shall make me scold you, if not hate you. What! An advocate for an impostor! Hush!

You think there are no more such shapes as he, having seen only him and Caliban: foolish girl! To most men this is a Caliban, and they to him are angels.

_Mir._ My affections

Are, then, most humble; I have no ambition

To see a more handsome man.

_Pros._ Come on; obey:

Your nerves are in their infancy again,

And have no strength in them.

_Fer._ So they are: my spirits, as in a dream, are all bound up.

My father’s loss, the weakness which I feel,

The loss of all my friends, and this man’s threats to whom I am now subject, mean little to me,

if only I might see this young woman once a day through my prison. Let liberty make use of every other corner of the earth; I have space enough

in such a prison.

_Pros._ [_Aside_] It is working. [_To Fer._] Come on. You have done well, fine Ariel! [_To Fer._] Follow me. [_To Ari._] Listen to what else you must do for me.

_Mir._ Be comforted; my father is of a better nature, sir,

than he appears by his speech. This is unusual

which now came from him.

_Pros._ You shall be as free

as mountain winds, but then you must exactly carry out

all points of my command.

_Ari._ To the syllable.

_Pros._ Come, follow. Do not speak on his behalf. [_Exeunt._

ACT II.

SCENE I. _Another part of the island._

_Enter ALONSO, SEBASTIAN, ANTONIO, GONZALO, ADRIAN, FRANCISCO, and others._

_Gon._ Please, sir, be cheerful. You have reason to be, as we all do—reason for joy. For our escape

is far greater than our loss. Our cause for sorrow

is common. Every day, some sailor’s wife,

the captains of some merchant ship, and the merchant himself,                      5 have just the same reason for grief as we do. But for the miracle—I mean our survival—few in millions

can speak like us. So, good sir, weigh wisely our sorrow against our comfort.

_Alon._                    Please, be quiet.

_Seb._ He receives comfort like cold porridge.                      10

_Ant._ The visitor won’t give up on him so easily.

_Seb._ Look, he’s winding up the watch of his wit; soon it will strike.

_Gon._ Sir,--

_Seb._ One: tell.                                                   15

_Gon._ When every grief that’s offered is accepted, it comes to the one who accepts it--

_Seb._ A dollar.

_Gon._ Sorrow comes to him, indeed. You have spoken truer than you intended.                                            20

_Seb._ You have taken it more wisely than I meant you to.

_Gon._ Therefore, my lord,--

_Ant._ Honestly, what a spendthrift he is with his tongue!

_Alon._ Please, spare us.

_Gon._ Well, I have finished. But yet,--                                25

_Seb._ He will keep talking.

_Ant._ Which one, he or Adrian, for a good wager, will start crowing first?

_Seb._ The old cock.

_Ant._ The young rooster.                                                30

_Seb._ Done. The wager?

_Ant._ A laugh.

_Seb._ A deal!

_Adr._ Though this island seems to be deserted,--

_Seb._ Ha, ha, ha!--So, you’re paid.                                35

_Adr._ Uninhabitable, and almost inaccessible,--

_Seb._ Yet,--

_Adr._ Yet,--

_Ant._ He couldn’t miss it.

_Adr._ It must be of a subtle, tender, and delicate              40 climate.

_Ant._ Temperance was a delicate girl.

_Seb._ Yes, and a subtle one; as he so learnedly explained.

_Adr._ The air breathes upon us here most sweetly.

_Seb._ As if it had lungs, and rotten ones.                         45

_Ant._ Or as if it were perfumed by a swamp.

_Gon._ Here is everything good for life.

_Ant._ True; except the means to live.

_Seb._ Of that there’s none, or little.

_Gon._ How lush and vigorous the grass looks! How green!               50

_Ant._ The ground, indeed, is brownish-yellow.

_Seb._ With a touch of green in it.

_Ant._ He doesn’t miss much.

_Seb._ No; he only mistakes the truth completely.

_Gon._ But the rarity of it is—which is indeed almost             55 beyond belief,--

_Seb._ As many attested rarities are.

_Gon._ Our clothes, even though they were soaked in the sea, still keep their freshness and shine—looking more newly dyed than stained with salt water.

_Ant._ If even one of his pockets could speak, wouldn’t it say he lies?

_Seb._ Yes, or very falsely pocket up his report.

_Gon._ It seems to me our clothes are now as fresh as when we first put them on in Africa, at the marriage of the king’s fair daughter Claribel to the King of Tunis.

_Seb._ It was a sweet marriage, and we prosper well in our return.

_Adr._ Tunis was never graced before with such a perfect queen.

_Gon._ Not since widow Dido’s time.

_Ant._ Widow! A pox on that! How did that widow come in? Widow Dido!

_Seb._ What if he had said “widower Aeneas” too? Good Lord, how you take it!

_Adr._ “Widow Dido,” did you say? You make me think about that: she was from Carthage, not Tunis.

_Gon._ This Tunis, sir, was Carthage.

_Adr._ Carthage?

_Gon._ I assure you, Carthage.

_Seb._ His word is more powerful than the miraculous harp; he has raised the wall, and houses too.

_Ant._ What impossible thing will he make easy next?

_Seb._ I think he will carry this island home in his pocket and give it to his son for an apple.

_Ant._ And, sowing the seeds of it in the sea, bring forth more islands.

_Gon._ Yes.

_Ant._ Why, in good time.

_Gon._ Sir, we were saying that our clothes seem now as fresh as when we were at Tunis at the marriage of your daughter, who is now queen.

_Ant._ And the rarest woman that ever came there.

_Seb._ Except, I beg you, widow Dido.

_Ant._ Oh, widow Dido! Yes, widow Dido.

_Gon._ Is not, sir, my doublet as fresh as the first day I wore it? I mean, in a way.

_Ant._ That “way” was well fished for.

_Gon._ When I wore it at your daughter’s marriage?

_Alon._ You cram these words into my ears against the stomach of my sense. I wish I had never

married my daughter there! For, coming from there, my son is lost, and, in my judgment, she too.

She is so far removed from Italy

that I shall never see her again. Oh, you, my heir of Naples and of Milan, what strange fish

has made its meal on you?

_Fran._ Sir, he may be alive:

I saw him beat the waves beneath him

and ride upon their backs; he trod the water.

He flung aside the waves’ hostility and breasted the most swollen surge that met him; he kept his bold head above the contentious waves, and rowed himself with his strong arms in vigorous strokes

toward the shore, which bent over its wave-worn base as if stooping to rescue him: I do not doubt he came alive to land.

_Alon._ No, no, he’s gone.

_Seb._ Sir, you may thank yourself for this great loss, since you would not bless our Europe with your daughter, but rather chose to lose her to an African;

where she is at least banished from your sight, and you have cause to weep for it.

_Alon._ Please, peace.

_Seb._ You were knelt to and begged otherwise by all of us; and the fair soul herself

weighed between reluctance and obedience, at

which end of the balance should bow. We have lost your son, I fear, forever: Milan and Naples have

more widows in them from this business’ making

than we bring men to comfort them:

the fault is your own.

_Alon._ So is the dearest part of what we’ve lost.

_Gon._ My lord Sebastian, the truth you speak lacks some gentleness, and you speak it at the wrong time: you rub the sore,

when you should bring the plaster.

_Seb._ Very well.

_Ant._ And most like a surgeon.

_Gon._ It is foul weather in us all, good sir, when you are cloudy.

_Seb._ Foul weather?

_Ant._ Very foul.

_Gon._ Had I the planting of this isle, my lord,--

_Ant._ He’d sow it with nettle-seed.

_Seb._ Or docks, or mallows.

_Gon._ And were I king of it, what would I do?

_Seb._ Escape being drunk for want of wine.

_Gon._ In my commonwealth I would do all things by contraries; for I would admit no kind of trade;

no name of magistrate;

letters should not be known; riches, poverty,

and use of service, none; contract, succession, boundary, limit of land, tillage, vineyard, none;

no use of metal, corn, or wine, or oil;

no occupation; all men idle, all;

and women too, but innocent and pure;

no sovereignty;--

_Seb._ Yet he would be king of it.

_Ant._ The latter end of his commonwealth forgets the beginning.

_Gon._ All things in common nature should produce without sweat or effort: treason, felony,

sword, pike, knife, gun, or need of any weapon, would I not have; but nature should bring forth, of its own kind, all plenty, all abundance,

to feed my innocent people.

_Seb._ No marrying among his subjects?

_Ant._ None, man; all idle; whores and knaves.

_Gon._ I would govern with such perfection, sir, as to excel the golden age.

_Seb._ God save his majesty!

_Ant._ Long live Gonzalo!

_Gon._ And,--do you mark me, sir?

_Alon._ Please, no more: you talk nothing to me.

_Gon._ I well believe your highness; and I did it to give occasion to these gentlemen, who are of such sensitive and nimble lungs that they always use to laugh at nothing.

_Ant._ It was you we laughed at.

_Gon._ Who in this kind of merry fooling am nothing to you: so you may continue, and laugh at nothing still.

_Ant._ What a blow was there given!

_Seb._ If it had not fallen flat-long.

_Gon._ You are gentlemen of brave mettle; you would lift the moon out of her sphere, if she would continue in it five weeks without changing.

_Enter ARIEL (invisible) playing solemn music._

_Seb._ We would so, and then go a bat-fowling.

_Ant._ Nay, good my lord, be not angry.

_Gon._ No, I warrant you; I will not risk my discretion so weakly. Will you laugh me asleep, for I am very heavy?

_Ant._ Go sleep, and hear us.

[_All sleep except Alon., Seb., and Ant._

_Alon._ What, all so soon asleep! I wish my eyes would, with themselves, shut up my thoughts: I find they are inclined to do so.

_Seb._ Please you, sir,

do not omit the heavy offer of it: it seldom visits sorrow; when it does,

it is a comforter.

_Ant._ We two, my lord,

will guard your person while you take your rest, and watch your safety.

_Alon._ Thank you.--Wondrous heavy. [_Alonso sleeps. Exit Ariel._

_Seb._ What a strange drowsiness possesses them!

_Ant._ It’s the nature of the climate.

_Seb._ Why?

Doesn’t it make our eyelids heavy? I don’t find

Myself inclined to sleep.

_Ant._ Nor do I; my spirits are lively. They all fell together, as if by agreement;

They dropped, as if struck by lightning. What might be, worthy Sebastian?—O, what might be?—No more:—

And yet I think I see it in your face,

What you should become: the opportunity calls to you; and my strong imagination sees a crown

Dropping upon your head.

_Seb._ What, are you awake?

_Ant._ Don’t you hear me speaking?

_Seb._ I do; and surely it is a sleepy language, and you speak

Out of your sleep. What was it you said?

This is a strange rest, to be asleep

With eyes wide open; standing, speaking, moving, and yet so fast asleep.

_Ant._ Noble Sebastian,

You’re letting your fortune sleep—die, rather; you close your eyes while you are waking.

_Seb._ You are snoring distinctly; there’s meaning in your snores.

_Ant._ I am more serious than I usually am: you must be so too, if you listen to me; which, if you do,

Will triple your worth.

_Seb._ Well, I am standing water.

_Ant._ I’ll teach you how to flow.

_Seb._ Do so: to ebb is what hereditary laziness teaches me.

_Ant._ O,

If you only knew how you cherish the purpose even while you mock it! How, in stripping it away, you invest it more! Ebbing men, indeed,

Most often run close to the bottom

Because of their own fear or laziness.

_Seb._ Please, go on:

The look of your eye and cheek proclaims a matter from you; and a birth, indeed,

Which pains you greatly to deliver.

_Ant._ Thus, sir:

Although this lord of weak memory, this one,

Who shall be of as little memory

When he is buried, has almost persuaded—for he’s a spirit of persuasion, only

Claims to persuade—the king that his son is alive, it is as impossible that he is not drowned

As that the one who sleeps here swims.

_Seb._ I have no hope

That he is not drowned.

_Ant._ O, out of that “no hope”

What great hope you have! No hope that way is

Another way so high a hope that even

Ambition cannot see a wink beyond, but doubts discovery there. Will you agree with me that Ferdinand is drowned?

_Seb._ He’s gone.

_Ant._ Then, tell me, who’s the next heir of Naples?

_Seb._ Claribel.

_Ant._ She who is queen of Tunis; she who lives ten leagues beyond human reach; she who from Naples can receive no message, unless the sun were a messenger—

The man in the moon is too slow—until newborn chins are rough and ready for shaving; she from whom

We all were swallowed by the sea, though some were cast back again, and by that destiny, to perform an act

Of which what’s past is prologue; what’s to come, is in your and my discharge.

_Seb._ What nonsense is this! What are you saying? It’s true, my brother’s daughter is queen of Tunis; so she is heir of Naples; and between those regions there is some distance.

_Ant._ A distance whose every cubit

Seems to cry out, “How shall that Claribel

Measure us back to Naples? Keep in Tunis, and let Sebastian wake.” Say, this were death

That has now seized them; why, they were no worse than they are now. There are men who can rule Naples as well as this sleeping king; lords who can chatter as long and pointlessly as this Gonzalo; I myself could make

A jackdaw of equally deep conversation. Oh, if only you had

The same mind as I do! What a sleep this would be

For your advancement! Do you understand me?

_Seb._ I think I do.

_Ant._ And how does your contentment look after your own good fortune?

_Seb._ I remember

You supplanted your brother Prospero.

_Ant._ True:

And look how well my clothes fit me now;

Much better than before. My brother’s servants were then my equals; now they are my men.

_Seb._ But what about your conscience?

_Ant._ Ah, sir, where is that? If it were a chilblain, it would force me to wear a slipper; but I do not feel

This deity in my chest. Twenty consciences,

If they stood between me and Milan, let them be frozen like candy, and melt before they trouble me! Here lies your brother, no better than the earth he lies upon,

If he were what he now resembles—that is, dead—whom I, with this obedient steel, just three inches of it, can put to bed forever; while you, doing the same, could put to eternal sleep

This ancient morsel, this Sir Prudence, who

Should not criticize our plan. As for all the rest, they will accept any suggestion as a cat laps milk;

They will set the clock to any business that we say suits the hour.

_Seb._ Your case, dear friend,

Shall be my example; just as you gained Milan,

I will gain Naples. Draw your sword: one stroke shall free you from the tribute you pay; and I, the king, shall love you.

_Ant._ Draw together; and when I raise my hand, you do the same,

To strike it down on Gonzalo.

_Seb._ Oh, but one word. [_They talk apart._

_Re-enter ARIEL invisible._

_Ari._ My master through his art foresees the danger that you, his friend, are in; and sends me forth—for otherwise his plan dies—to keep them alive. [_Sings in Gonzalo’s ear._

While you here lie snoring,

Open-eyed conspiracy

Takes its opportunity.

If you care for your life,

Shake off slumber, and beware: Awake, awake!

_Ant._ Then let us both act quickly.

_Gon._ Now, good angels preserve the king! [_They wake._

_Alon._ Why, what is this? Ho, awake!—Why are your swords drawn? Why these ghastly looks?

_Gon._ What is the matter?

_Seb._ While we stood here guarding your rest, just now, we heard a hollow burst of bellowing like bulls, or rather lions: did it not wake you? It struck my ear most terribly.

_Alon._ I heard nothing.

_Ant._ Oh, it was a din to frighten a monster’s ear, enough to make an earthquake! Surely it was the roar

Of a whole herd of lions.

_Alon._ Did you hear this, Gonzalo?

SCENE II. _Another part of the island._

_Enter CALIBAN carrying a load of wood. A noise of thunder is heard._

_Caliban._ May all the infections that the sun sucks up from bogs, marshes, and flats fall on Prosper, and make him sick inch by inch! His spirits hear me,

And yet I must curse. But they will not pinch me, frighten me with goblin-shows, throw me into the mud,

Or lead me, like a will-o'-the-wisp, in the dark out of my way, unless he commands them. But

For every little thing they are set upon me.

Sometimes like apes, they make faces and chatter at me, and afterwards bite me. Then like hedgehogs, which lie tumbling in my barefoot path and raise

Their spines at my footstep. Sometimes I am

All wrapped around with adders, who with forked tongues hiss me into madness.

_Enter TRINCULO._

Look, now, look!

Here comes a spirit of his, and to torment me for bringing wood in slowly. I'll fall flat;

Perhaps he will not notice me.

_Trinculo._ There is neither bush nor shrub here to shelter from any weather at all, and another storm is brewing. I hear it singing in the wind. That same black cloud, that huge one, looks like a foul jug that would pour out its liquid. If it should thunder as it did before, I do not know where to hide my head. That same cloud cannot help but fall by the bucketful. What have we here? A man or a fish? Dead or alive? A fish: he smells like a fish; a very ancient and fish-like smell; a kind of not-of-the-newest salted fish. A strange fish! If I were in England now, as I once was, and had only this fish painted, not a holiday fool there but would give a piece of silver. There this monster would make a man's fortune; any strange beast there makes a man's fortune. When they will not give a penny to relieve a lame beggar, they will lay out ten to see a dead Indian. Legged like a man! And his fins like arms! Warm, on my word! I do now let loose my opinion and hold it no longer: this is no fish, but an islander who has lately suffered from a thunderbolt. [_Thunder._] Alas, the storm has come again! My best way is to creep under his cloak; there is no other shelter nearby. Misery acquaints a man with strange bedfellows. I will hide here until the worst of the storm is past.

_Enter STEPHANO, singing: a bottle in his hand._

_Stephano._ I shall go no more to sea, to sea; here shall I die ashore--

This is a very wretched tune to sing at a man's funeral. Well, here is my comfort. [_Drinks._]

[_Sings._] The master, the deck-scrubber, the boatswain, and I, the gunner, and his mate, loved Mall, Meg, and Marian, and Margery, but none of us cared for Kate. For she had a tongue with a sting, and would cry to a sailor, "Go hang!" She loved not the smell of tar nor of pitch, yet a tailor might scratch her wherever she itched. Then, to sea, boys, and let her go hang!

This is a wretched tune too: but here is my comfort. [_Drinks._]

_Caliban._ Do not torment me:--O!

Stephano: What’s the matter? Are there devils here? Are you playing tricks on us with savages and men from India? I haven’t escaped drowning just to be afraid of your four legs now. For it has been said, “As proper a man as ever went on four legs cannot make him give ground.” And it shall be said again, while Stephano breathes through his nostrils.

Caliban: The spirit torments me—O!

Stephano: This is some monster of the island with four legs, who has caught, I think, a fever. Where the devil did he learn our language? I will give him some help, if only for that. Caliban: Do not torment me, please; I’ll bring my wood home faster.

Stephano: He’s in a fit now, and doesn’t talk very sensibly. He shall taste from my bottle. If he has never drunk wine before, it will go a long way toward curing his fit. Caliban: You have done me only a little hurt so far; soon you will do more. I know it by your trembling: now Prospero works upon you.

ACT III.

SCENE I. _Before PROSPERO'S cell._

_Enter FERDINAND, bearing a log._

_Fer._ Some sports are painful, but the labor involved makes the delight in them stand out more. Some lowly tasks are undertaken nobly, and even the poorest matters

can lead to rich outcomes. This humble task of mine

would be as burdensome to me as it is hateful, but the mistress I serve brings life to what is dead and makes my labors pleasures. Oh, she is

ten times more gentle than her father is harsh. And he is made entirely of severity. I must move

some thousands of these logs and pile them up, under a harsh command. My sweet mistress

weeps when she sees me work, and says such lowly work never had such a worker before. I forget myself:

but these sweet thoughts do indeed refresh my labors, when I am busiest, least when I am actually doing it.

_Enter MIRANDA; and PROSPERO at a distance, unseen._

_Mir._ Alas, now, please, do not work so hard. I wish the lightning had

burned up those logs that you are ordered to pile! Please, set it down and rest. When this log burns, it will weep for having wearied you. My father

is deep in study; please, rest now. He is safely occupied for these three hours.

_Fer._ Oh, most dear mistress, the sun will set before I can finish

what I must strive to do.

_Mir._ If you will sit down,

I will carry your logs for a while. Please, give me that; I will carry it to the pile.

_Fer._ No, precious creature, I would rather crack my sinews, break my back,

than have you undergo such dishonor

while I sit lazily by.

_Mir._ It would suit me

as well as it suits you: and I would do it

with much more ease, because my good will is in it, while yours is against it.

_Pros._ Poor worm, you are infected! This visit shows it.

_Mir._ You look weary.

_Fer._ No, noble mistress; it is fresh morning to me when you are by at night. I beg you--

chiefly so that I might include it in my prayers--

what is your name?

_Mir._ Miranda. Oh, my father, I have broken your command to say so!

_Fer._ Admired Miranda! Indeed the very height of admiration! Worth

what is dearest in the world! Many a lady

I have looked upon with highest regard, and many a time the harmony of their voices has brought my too-eager ear into bondage. For various virtues I have liked various women; never any

with so full a soul, but some defect in her

would quarrel with the noblest grace she possessed and defeat it. But you, oh you,

so perfect and so peerless, are created

from the best of every creature!

_Mir._ I do not know

a single one of my own sex; I remember no woman's face,

except, from my mirror, my own. Nor have I seen more that I may call men than you, good friend, and my dear father. What faces look like elsewhere,

I have no knowledge of; but, by my modesty,

The jewel in my dowry, I would not wish

Any companion in the world but you; nor can imagination form a shape,

Besides yourself, to like of. But I prattle

Something too wildly, and my father’s precepts I therein do forget.

_Fer._ I am, in my condition,

A prince, Miranda; I do think, a king; I would, not so!--and would no more endure

This wooden slavery than to suffer

The flesh-fly blow my mouth. Hear my soul speak: the very instant that I saw you, did

My heart fly to your service; there resides, to make me slave to it; and for your sake

Am I this patient log-man.

_Mir._ Do you love me?

_Fer._ O heaven, O earth, bear witness to this sound, and crown what I profess with kind event,

If I speak true! if hollowly, invert what best is boded me to mischief! I,

Beyond all limit of what else in the world,

Do love, prize, honour you.

_Mir._ I am a fool

To weep at what I am glad of.

_Pros._ Fair encounter

Of two most rare affections! Heavens rain grace on that which breeds between them!

_Fer._ Wherefore weep you?

_Mir._ At my unworthiness, that dare not offer what I desire to give; and much less take

What I shall die to want. But this is trifling; and all the more it seeks to hide itself, the bigger bulk it shows. Hence, bashful cunning! And prompt me, plain and holy innocence!

I am your wife, if you will marry me;

If not, I’ll die your maid: to be your fellow

You may deny me; but I’ll be your servant, whether you will or no.

_Fer._ My mistress, dearest;

And I thus humble ever.

_Mir._ My husband, then?

_Fer._ Ay, with a heart as willing

As bondage ever was of freedom: here’s my hand.

_Mir._ And mine, with my heart in it: and now farewell till half an hour hence.

_Fer._ A thousand thousand!

[_Exeunt Fer. and Mir. severally._

_Pros._ So glad of this as they I cannot be,

Who are surprised withal; but my rejoicing

At nothing can be more. I’ll to my book;

For yet, before supper-time, must I perform much business appertaining. [_Exit._

SCENE III. _Another part of the island._

_Enter ALONSO, SEBASTIAN, ANTONIO, GONZALO, ADRIAN, FRANCISCO, and others._

_Gon._ By our Lady, I can go no further, sir;

My old bones ache: here is a maze we have walked through, indeed, with straight paths and winding ones! With your patience, I must rest.

_Alon._ Old lord, I cannot blame you, for I myself am seized with weariness, to the dulling of my spirits: sit down, and rest. Here I will give up my hope, and no longer keep it as my flatterer: he is drowned

Our human generation you shall find

ACT IV.

SCENE I. Before PROSPERO'S cell.

Enter PROSPERO, FERDINAND, and MIRANDA.

Prospero: If I have punished you too harshly,

Your reward makes up for it; for I

Have given you here a third of my own life,

Or that for which I live; and once again

I place her in your hand: all your troubles were only my tests of your love, and you

Have remarkably stood the test: here, before Heaven, I confirm this rich gift of mine. Oh Ferdinand,

Do not smile at me because I praise her so highly,

For you will find she will surpass all praise, and make it fall short behind her.

Ferdinand: I do believe it,

Even against an oracle.

Prospero: Then, as my gift, and your own acquisition worthily purchased, take my daughter: but

If you break her virgin-knot before

All sacred ceremonies may with full and holy rite be performed,

No sweet blessing shall the heavens let fall

To make this contract grow; but barren hatred,

Sour-eyed disdain and conflict will scatter the union of your bed with weeds so hateful

That you will hate it entirely. So take care, as surely as Hymen's lamps will light you.

_Fer._ As I hope

For peaceful days, healthy children, and long life,

With such love as I feel now, the darkest den, the most convenient place, the strongest temptation our worse nature can offer, shall never melt

My honor into lust, to take away

The joy of that day's celebration

When I shall think either Phoebus' horses are exhausted, or Night kept chained below.

_Pros._ Well spoken.

Sit, then, and talk with her; she is yours. What, Ariel! My hard-working servant, Ariel!

_Enter ARIEL._

_Ari._ What does my powerful master want? Here I am.

_Pros._ You and your lesser spirits performed your last service worthily; and I must use you

In another such trick. Go bring the rabble,

Over whom I give you power, here to this place. Urge them to move quickly; for I must

Present to the eyes of this young couple some display of my art. It is my promise,

And they expect it from me.

_Ari._ Right now?

_Pros._ Yes, in an instant.

_Ari._ Before you can say "come" and "go,"

And breathe twice, and cry "so, so,"

Each one, tripping on his toes,

Will be here with funny faces and grimaces.

Do you love me, master? No?

_Pros._ Dearly, my delicate Ariel. Do not come near until you hear me call.

_Ari._ Well, I understand. [_Exit._

_Pros._ Be careful to stay true; do not give affection too much freedom. The strongest promises are like straw to the fire in the blood. Be more restrained,

Or else, farewell to your vow!

_Fer._ I assure you, sir; the white cold virgin snow upon my heart cools the heat of my passion.

_Pros._ Well.

Now come, my Ariel! Bring an extra spirit rather than lack one. Appear, and be lively! No talking! All eyes! Be silent. [_Soft music._

_Enter IRIS._

_Iris._ Ceres, most generous lady, your rich fields of wheat, rye, barley, vetch, oats, and peas; your grassy mountains, where nibbling sheep live, and flat meadows covered with straw to keep them; your banks with trimmed and woven edges,

Which rainy April decorates at your command, to make chaste crowns for cold nymphs; and your broom groves, whose shade the rejected bachelor loves, being lovesick; your vine-clipped vineyard;

And your seashore, barren and rocky-hard, where you yourself take the air—the queen of the sky, whose watery rainbow and messenger I am,

Bids you leave these; and with her royal grace, here on this grass plot, in this very place, to come and play. Her peacocks fly swiftly. Approach, rich Ceres, to entertain her.

_Enter CERES._

_Cer._ Hail, many-colored messenger, who never disobeys the wife of Jupiter;

Who, with your saffron wings, upon my flowers scatter honey-drops and refreshing showers; and with each end of your blue bow crown my wooded acres and my open, shrubless hills,

A rich scarf for my proud earth—why has your queen summoned me here, to this short-grassed green?

_Iris._ To celebrate a contract of true love; and to bestow some gift freely upon the blessed lovers.

_Cer._ Tell me, heavenly bow,

If Venus or her son, as you well know,

Are now attending the queen? Since they plotted the way that dark Dis got my daughter,

I have sworn off her and her blind boy's disgraceful company.

_Iris._ Of her company

Do not be afraid: I met her goddess

Cutting through the clouds toward Paphos, and her son drawn by doves along with her. They had thought to work some lustful charm upon this man and maid, whose vows are that no bed-right shall be paid until Hymen's torch is lighted: but it was in vain; Mars's hot favorite has returned again;

Her irritable son has broken his arrows, swears he will shoot no more, but play with sparrows, and be a boy right out.

_Cer._ Highest queen of state, great Juno, comes; I know her by her walk.

_Enter JUNO._

_Juno._ How does my generous sister? Go with me to bless this couple, that they may be prosperous, and honored in their children. [_They sing:_

_Juno._ Honor, riches, marriage-blessing,

Long continuance, and increasing,

Hourly joys be still upon you!

Juno sings her blessings on you.

_Cer._ Earth's increase, abundant plenty, barns and granaries never empty;

Vines with clustering bunches growing; plants with goodly burden bowing; spring come to you at the farthest in the very end of harvest! Scarcity and want shall shun you;

Ceres' blessing so is on you.

_Fer._ This is a most majestic vision, and

Harmoniously charming. May I be bold

To think these are spirits?

_Pros._ Spirits, which by my art

I have called from their confines to enact

My present fancies.

_Fer._ Let me live here ever;

So rare a wondrous father and a wife

Makes this place Paradise.

[_Juno and Ceres whisper, and send Iris on employment._

_Pros._ Sweet, now, silence!

Juno and Ceres whisper seriously; there's something else to do: hush, and be mute, or else our spell is ruined.

_Iris._ You nymphs, called Naiads, of the winding brooks, with your sedged crowns and ever-harmless looks, leave your crisp channels, and on this green land answer your summons; Juno does command: come, temperate nymphs, and help to celebrate a contract of true love; be not too late.

_Enter certain Nymphs._

You sunburnt field-workers, weary of August, come here from the furrow, and be merry: make holiday; put on your rye-straw hats, and meet these fresh nymphs every one in country dancing.

_Enter certain Reapers, properly dressed: they join with the Nymphs in a graceful dance; toward the end of which PROSPERO starts suddenly, and speaks; after which, to a strange, hollow, and confused noise, they heavily vanish._

_Pros._ [_Aside_] I had forgotten that foul conspiracy of the beast Caliban and his confederates against my life: the minute of their plot

Is almost come. [_To the Spirits._] Well done! Leave; no more!

_Fer._ This is strange: your father is in some passion that works him strongly.

_Mir._ Never till this day

Saw I him touched with anger so distempered.

_Pros._ You do look, my son, in a moved manner,

As if you were dismayed: be cheerful, sir.

Our revels now are ended. These our actors,

As I foretold you, were all spirits, and

The cloud-capped towers, the gorgeous palaces, the solemn temples, the great globe itself,

Yes, all who inherit it, shall dissolve,

And, like this insubstantial pageant faded, leave not a trace behind. We are such stuff

As dreams are made of; and our little life

Is rounded with a sleep. Sir, I am troubled;

Bear with my weakness; my old brain is disturbed. Do not be upset by my infirmity. If you are willing, retire into my cell,

And rest there. I will walk a turn or two,

To still my beating mind.

Ferdinand. Miranda. We wish your peace. [They exit.]

Prospero. Come with a thought. I thank you, Ariel. Come.

Enter ARIEL.

Ariel. I cling to your thoughts. What is your pleasure?

Prospero. Spirit, we must prepare to meet with Caliban.

Ariel. Yes, my commander. When I presented Ceres, I thought to have told you of it, but I feared I might anger you.

Prospero. Say again, where did you leave these scoundrels?

Ariel. I told you, sir, they were red-hot with drinking; so full of courage that they struck the air

For breathing in their faces; beat the ground

For kissing their feet; yet always bending

Toward their plan. Then I beat my drum; at which, like unbroken colts, they pricked up their ears, opened their eyelids, lifted up their noses

As if they smelled music. So I charmed their ears,

That, like calves, they followed my lowing through toothed briers, sharp furze, prickly gorse, and thorns, which cut their frail shins. At last I left them in the filthy-mantled pool beyond your cell,

There dancing up to their chins, so that the foul lake stank worse than their feet.

Prospero. This was well done, my bird. Keep your shape invisible still. Bring the trumpery in my house here,

As bait to catch these thieves.

Ariel. I go, I go. [Exit.]

Prospero. A devil, a born devil, on whose nature nurture can never stick; on whom my efforts,

Humanely taken, are all, all lost, quite lost. And as with age his body grows uglier,

So his mind rots. I will plague them all,

Even to roaring.

Re-enter ARIEL, loaded with glittering apparel, etc.

Come, hang them on this line.

PROSPERO and ARIEL remain, invisible. Enter CALIBAN, STEPHANO, and TRINCULO, all wet.

Caliban. Please, tread softly, so that the blind mole may not hear a footfall. We are now near his cell.

Stephano. Monster, your fairy, which you say is a harmless fairy, has done little better than played tricks on us.

Trinculo. Monster, I smell nothing but horse-piss; at which my nose is in great indignation.

Stephano. So is mine. Do you hear, monster? If I should take a displeasure against you, look you—

Trinculo. You were but a lost monster.

Caliban. Good my lord, give me your favor still. Be patient, for the prize I will bring you to

Shall cover this mischance. Therefore speak softly. All is hushed as midnight yet.

Trinculo. Yes, but to lose our bottles in the pool—

Stephano. There is not only disgrace and dishonor in that, monster, but an infinite loss.

Trinculo. That matters more to me than my wetting. Yet this is your harmless fairy, monster.

Stephano. I will fetch my bottle, though I go over my ears for my labor.

Caliban: Please, my king, be quiet. Do you see this? This is the entrance to the cell. No noise, and go in. Do that good bit of mischief which may make this island yours forever, and I, your Caliban,

will be your foot-licker forever.

Stephano: Give me your hand. I am beginning to have bloody thoughts.

Trinculo: O King Stephano! O noble one! O worthy Stephano! Look what a wardrobe is here for you!

Caliban: Leave it alone, you fool; it is just trash.

Trinculo: O ho, monster! We know what belongs in a secondhand clothes shop. O King Stephano!

Stephano: Take off that gown, Trinculo. By this hand, I will have that gown.

Trinculo: Your Grace shall have it.

Caliban: May dropsy drown this fool! What do you mean by doting on such luggage? Leave it alone, and let us do the murder first. If he wakes,

from head to toe he will cover our skins with pinches and make us into strange things.

Stephano: Be quiet, monster. Mistress line, is this not my jacket? Now the jacket is under the line. Now, jacket, you are likely to lose your hair and become a bald jacket.

Trinculo: Do, do. We steal by line and level, if it please your Grace.

Stephano: I thank you for that joke. Here is a garment for it. Wit shall not go unrewarded while I am king of this country. “Steal by line and level” is an excellent trick of the brain. There is another garment for it.

Trinculo: Monster, come, put some lime on your fingers, and carry away the rest.

Caliban: I will have none of it. We shall lose our time, and all be turned into barnacles, or into apes

with villainously low foreheads.

Stephano: Monster, put your fingers to work. Help carry this away to where my barrel of wine is, or I will turn you out of my kingdom. Go on, carry this.

Trinculo: And this.

Stephano: Yes, and this.

A noise of hunters is heard. Enter various Spirits, in the shape of dogs and hounds, and hunt them about. PROSPERO and ARIEL set them on.

Prospero: Hey, Mountain, hey!

Ariel: Silver! There it goes, Silver!

Prospero: Fury, Fury! There, Tyrant, there! Listen, listen! Caliban, Stephano, and Trinculo are driven out.

Go command my goblins to grind their joints with dry convulsions, to shorten up their sinews

with aged cramps, and to make them more spotted with pinches than a leopard or a mountain cat.

Ariel: Listen, they roar!

Prospero: Let them be hunted thoroughly. At this hour all my enemies lie at my mercy.

Shortly all my labors shall end, and you

shall have the air in freedom. For a little while,

follow me and serve me. They exit.

ACT V.

SCENE I. _Before the cell of Prospero._

_Enter PROSPERO in his magic robes, and ARIEL._

_Pros._ Now my plan is reaching its climax.

My spells are holding firm; my spirits obey; and time is moving along smoothly with its burden. What time of day is it?

_Ari._ It is the sixth hour; at which time, my lord, you said our work should end.

_Pros._ I did say so, when I first raised the tempest. Tell me, my spirit, how are the king and his followers?

_Ari._ They are confined together in the same manner as you instructed,

just as you left them; all prisoners, sir,

in the lime-grove which shelters your cell from the weather. They cannot move until you release them. The king, his brother, and yours, are all three distracted, and the rest are mourning over them,

brimful of sorrow and dismay; but chiefly

him that you called, sir, “The good old lord, Gonzalo.” His tears run down his beard, like winter’s drops from the eaves of reed roofs. Your charm works on them so strongly that if you saw them now, your feelings

would become tender.

_Pros._ Do you think so, spirit?

_Ari._ Mine would, sir, if I were human.

And mine shall. You, who are only air, can feel and be touched by their suffering—so shouldn't I,

One of their own kind, who feels everything as sharply and passionately as they do, be moved even more kindly than you are? Though their great wrongs have wounded me deeply,

Yet I choose my nobler reason over my fury:

The rarer action is in virtue, not vengeance. Since they are penitent, the sole aim of my purpose extends

Not a frown further. Go release them, Ariel. I'll break my charms, restore their senses, and they shall be themselves again.

_Ari._ I'll fetch them, sir. [_Exit._

_Pros._ You elves of hills, brooks, standing lakes, and groves; and you who on the sands with printless foot

Chase the ebbing Neptune, and flee from him when he comes back; you little puppets who

By moonshine make the green sour rings in the grass,

Which the ewe does not bite; and you whose pastime is to make midnight mushrooms, who rejoice

To hear the solemn curfew; by whose aid—weak masters though you are—I have dimmed

The noontide sun, called forth the mutinous winds, and between the green sea and the blue vault of sky

Set roaring war. To the dread rattling thunder I have given fire, and split Jove's stout oak

With his own bolt. The strong-based promontory I have made shake, and by the roots plucked up the pine and cedar. Graves at my command

Have woken their sleepers, opened, and let them forth by my so potent art. But this rough magic

I here renounce; and, when I have required some heavenly music—which even now I do—

To work my purpose upon their senses, which this airy charm is for, I'll break my staff,

Bury it certain fathoms in the earth, and deeper than any plummet has ever sounded

I'll drown my book. [_Solemn music._]

_Re-enter ARIEL before: then ALONSO, with a frantic gesture, attended by GONZALO; SEBASTIAN and ANTONIO in like manner, attended by ADRIAN and FRANCISCO: they all enter the circle which PROSPERO had made, and there stand charmed; which PROSPERO observing, speaks:_

A solemn melody, and the best comforter

To an unsettled mind, cure your brains,

Now useless, boiling within your skull! There stand, for you are spell-stopped.

Holy Gonzalo, honorable man,

My eyes, even sympathetic to the sight of yours, fall with companionable tears. The charm dissolves apace; and as the morning steals upon the night,

Melting the darkness, so their rising senses begin to chase the ignorant fumes that cloud

Their clearer reason. O good Gonzalo,

My true preserver, and a loyal servant

To him you follow! I will repay your kindness fully both in word and deed. Most cruelly

Did you, Alonso, use me and my daughter.

Your brother was a furtherer in the act.

You are punished for it now, Sebastian. Flesh and blood, you, brother mine, who entertained ambition,

Expelled remorse and nature; who, with Sebastian—whose inward torments therefore are most strong—would here have killed your king. I do forgive you, unnatural though you are. Their understanding begins to swell; and the approaching tide will shortly fill the shore of reason,

You still look at me, or would know me. Ariel, fetch me the hat and rapier from my cell.

I will take off these robes and present myself as I was when I was Duke of Milan. Quickly, spirit;

you shall soon be free.

_ARIEL sings and helps to dress him._

Where the bee sucks, there I suck:

In a cowslip's bell I lie;

There I rest when owls cry. On the bat's back I do fly

After summer, merrily.

Merrily, merrily shall I live now

Under the blossom that hangs on the bough.

_Pros._ Why, that's my delicate Ariel! I shall miss you; but yet you shall have freedom. So, so, so.

Go to the king's ship, invisible as you are.

There you shall find the sailors asleep

under the hatches. The master and the boatswain are awake; make them come here, and do it at once, please.

_Ari._ I drink the air before me, and return

before your pulse beats twice. [_Exit._

_Gon._ All torment, trouble, wonder, and amazement live here. May some heavenly power guide us

out of this fearful country!

_Pros._ Behold, sir king, the wronged Duke of Milan, Prospero.

For more assurance that a living prince

now speaks to you, I embrace your body;

and to you and your company I bid

a hearty welcome.

_Alon._ Whether you are he or not, or some enchanted illusion to deceive me,

as I lately have been, I do not know. Your pulse

beats as if you are flesh and blood; and since I saw you, the affliction of my mind improves, which,

I fear, was a madness holding me. This must require—if this is real at all—a most strange story.

I resign your dukedom, and I beg

you to pardon me my wrongs. But how could Prospero be living and be here?

_Pros._ First, noble friend, let me embrace your age, whose honour cannot

be measured or confined.

_Gon._ Whether this is real

or not, I will not swear.

_Pros._ You still taste

some subtleties of the isle that will not let you believe things certain. Welcome, my friends all! [_Aside to Seb. and Ant._]

But you, my pair of lords, if I were so inclined, I could here bring his Highness's frown upon you and prove you traitors. At this time

I will tell no tales.

_Seb._ [_Aside_] The devil speaks in him.

_Pros._ No. For you, most wicked sir, whom to call brother would even infect my mouth, I do forgive

your foulest fault—all of them—and require

my dukedom of you, which by force, I know,

you must restore.

_Alon._ If you are Prospero,

give us the details of your survival; how you have met us here, who three hours since were wrecked upon this shore; where I have lost—how sharp the point of this remembrance is!—

my dear son Ferdinand.

_Pros._ I am sorry for it, sir.

_Alon._ The loss is irreparable; and patience

says it is past her cure.

_Pros._ I rather think you have not sought her help, whose soft grace for a similar loss I have found to be a sovereign aid,

and I rest content.

_Alon._ You had a similar loss!

_Pros._ As great to me, and recent; and to make the dear loss supportable, I have means much weaker

than you may call on to comfort you, for I have lost my daughter.

_Alon._ A daughter?

O heavens, if only they were both living in Naples, the king and queen there! If they were, I wish I myself were buried in that muddy bed

Where my son lies. When did you lose your daughter?

_Pros._ In this last tempest. I see these lords are so astonished at this meeting

That they are swallowing their own reason, and scarcely think

Their eyes are telling the truth, or that their words

Are natural breath. But however much you have been jolted from your senses, know for certain that I am Prospero, and that very duke

Who was driven out of Milan; who most strangely landed on this shore, where you were shipwrecked, to be its lord. No more of this for now;

For it is a story to be told day by day,

Not a tale for a breakfast, nor

Fitting this first meeting. Welcome, sir; this cell is my court. Here I have few attendants, and no subjects abroad. Please, look in.

Since you have given me my dukedom back again,

I will repay you with something just as good;

At least I will bring forth a wonder to satisfy you as much as my dukedom satisfies me.

_Here Prospero reveals FERDINAND and MIRANDA playing at chess._

_Mir._ Sweet lord, you are cheating.

_Fer._ No, my dearest love, I would not do that for the world.

_Mir._ Yes, for a score of kingdoms you would argue, and I would call it fair play.

_Alon._ If this proves to be a vision of the island, I shall lose one dear son

Twice.

_Seb._ A most high miracle!

_Fer._ Though the seas threaten, they are merciful; I have cursed them without cause. [_Kneels._]

_Alon._ Now may all the blessings of a glad father surround you! Arise, and tell me how you came here.

_Mir._ O, wonder!

How many goodly creatures there are here!

How beautiful mankind is! O brave new world,

That has such people in it!

_Pros._ It is new to you.

_Alon._ Who is this maiden with whom you were playing? Your oldest acquaintance cannot be more than three hours. Is she the goddess who has separated us,

And brought us thus together?

_Fer._ Sir, she is mortal; but by immortal Providence she is mine.

I chose her when I could not ask my father for his advice, nor did I think I had one. She

Is daughter to this famous Duke of Milan,

Of whom I have so often heard renown,

But never saw before; from whom I have

Received a second life; and this lady makes him a second father to me.

_Alon._ I am hers.

But, O, how strange it will sound that I

Must ask my child for forgiveness!

_Pros._ There, sir, stop. Let us not burden our memories with

A heaviness that is gone.

_Gon._ I have wept inwardly, or I would have spoken before this. Look down, you gods, and on this couple drop a blessed crown!

For it is you who have chalked out the way

Which brought us here.

_Alon._ I say, Amen, Gonzalo!

_Gon._ Was Milan driven from Milan, so that his children

Should become kings of Naples? O, rejoice beyond a common joy! And set it down

With gold on lasting pillars: In one voyage

Did Claribel find her husband at Tunis,

And Ferdinand, her brother, found a wife where he himself was lost, Prospero found his dukedom in a poor isle, and all of us found ourselves.

When no man was his own master.

_Alon._ [_to Fer. and Mir._] Give me your hands: let grief and sorrow forever grip the heart

of anyone who does not wish you joy!

_Gon._ So be it! Amen!

_Re-enter ARIEL, with the Master and Boatswain following in amazement._

Oh, look, sir, look, sir! Here are more of us:

I predicted that if there were a gallows on land,

this fellow could not drown. Now, you blasphemer,

who swore grace overboard, have you no oaths on shore? Have you no mouth on land? What is the news?

_Boats._ The best news is that we have safely found our king and company; the next, our ship--

which, only three hours ago, we reported split-- is tight and ready and splendidly rigged, just as when

we first put out to sea.

_Ari._ [_Aside to Pros._] Sir, I have done all this service since I left.

_Pros._ [_Aside to Ari._] My clever spirit!

_Alon._ These are not natural events; they grow stranger and stranger. Say, how did you come here?

_Boats._ If I believed, sir, that I was truly awake, I would try to tell you. We were dead asleep, and-- somehow, we know not how--all shut under the hatches; where, just now, with strange and various noises of roaring, shrieking, howling, jingling chains, and more diversity of sounds, all horrible,

we were awakened; immediately, we were free;

where we saw our royal, good, and gallant ship in all her trim; our master

dancing to see her:--in an instant, if you please, as if in a dream, we were separated from them,

and were brought here in a daze.

_Ari._ [_Aside to Pros._] Was it well done?

_Pros._ [_Aside to Ari._] Splendidly, my diligent one. You shall be free.

_Alon._ This is as strange a maze as men have ever walked; and there is more in this business than nature ever directed: some oracle

must set our understanding right.

_Pros._ Sir, my liege, do not trouble your mind by dwelling on

the strangeness of this business; at a chosen time, which shall be soon, I will explain to you alone, in a way that will seem believable, every one of

these events that have happened; until then, be cheerful,

and think well of each thing.

[_Aside to Ari._] Come here, spirit:

set Caliban and his companions free; undo the spell. [_Exit Ariel._] How is my gracious sir? There are still missing from your company

a few odd fellows that you do not remember.

_Re-enter ARIEL, driving in CALIBAN, STEPHANO, and TRINCULO, in their stolen clothes._

_Ste._ Every man shift for all the rest, and let no man take care for himself; for all is but fortune. --Courage, bully-monster, courage!

_Trin._ If these are true eyes I wear in my head, here is a goodly sight.

_Cal._ O Setebos, these are fine spirits indeed! How fine my master is! I am afraid

he will punish me.

_Seb._ Ha, ha!

What things are these, my lord Antonio?

Will money buy them?

_Ant._ Very likely; one of them is plainly a fish, and, no doubt, marketable.

_Pros._ Mark but the badges of these men, my lords, then say if they be true. This misshapen knave-- his mother was a witch, and one so strong

that she could control the moon, make tides and ebbs, and act in her command without her power.

These three have robbed me; and this half-devil-- for he is a bastard one--had plotted with them

To take my life. Two of these men you

Must know and recognize; this creature of darkness I admit is mine.

_Cal._ I shall be pinched to death.

_Alon._ Is this not Stephano, my drunken butler?

_Seb._ He is drunk now: where did he get wine?

_Alon._ And Trinculo is staggering drunk: where could they find this great liquor that has gilded them? How did you get into this state?

_Trin._ I have been in such a state since I last saw you that, I fear, it will never leave my bones: I shall not fear being fly-blown.

_Seb._ Why, how now, Stephano!

_Ste._ O, do not touch me; I am not Stephano, but a cramp.

_Pros._ You wanted to be king of the isle, sirrah?

_Ste._ I would have been a sore king, then.

_Alon._ This is as strange a thing as I have ever looked on. [_Pointing to Caliban._

_Pros._ He is as misshapen in his manners as in his body. Go, sirrah, to my cell;

Take your companions with you; if you hope

To receive my pardon, make it neat and tidy.

_Cal._ Yes, that I will; and I will be wise from now on, and seek for grace. What a thrice-double ass was I, to take this drunkard for a god,

And worship this dull fool!

_Pros._ Go on; away!

_Alon._ Go, and put your stolen goods back where you found them.

_Seb._ Or stole them, rather. [_Exeunt Cal., Ste., and Trin._

_Pros._ Sir, I invite your Highness and your company to my poor cell, where you shall rest for this one night; part of it I will spend with such conversation as, I do not doubt, will make it pass quickly: the story of my life,

And the particular events that have happened since I came to this isle: and in the morning

I will bring you to your ship, and so to Naples, where I hope to see the wedding

Of these our dear-beloved ones solemnized;

And then I will retire to my Milan, where every third thought shall be of my grave.

_Alon._ I long

To hear the story of your life, which must

Captivate the ear strangely.

_Pros._ I will tell it all;

And promise you calm seas, favorable winds,

And such swift sailing that you shall catch

Your royal fleet far off. [_Aside to Ari._] My Ariel, chick, that is your task: then to the elements

Be free, and farewell! Please you, come near. [_Exeunt._

EPILOGUE.

SPOKEN BY PROSPERO.

Now my charms are all o’erthrown,

And what strength I have’s mine own,

Which is most faint: now, ’tis true,

I must be here confined by you,

Or sent to Naples. Let me not, 5 Since I have my dukedom got,

And pardon’d the deceiver, dwell

In this bare island by your spell;

But release me from my bands

With the help of your good hands: 10 Gentle breath of yours my sails

Must fill, or else my project fails,

Which was to please. Now I want

Spirits to enforce, art to enchant;

And my ending is despair, 15 Unless I be relieved by prayer,

Which pierces so, that it assaults

Mercy itself, and frees all faults.

As you from crimes would pardon’d be,

Let your indulgence set me free. 20

NOTES.

NOTE I.

I. 1. 15. _What cares these roarers._ This grammatical inaccuracy, which escaped correction in the later folios, probably came from Shakespeare’s pen. Similar cases occur frequently, especially when the verb precedes its nominative. For example, _Tempest_, IV. 1. 262, ‘Lies at my mercy all mine enemies,’ and _Measure for Measure_, II. 1. 22, ‘What knows the laws, &c.’ We correct it in those passages where the occurrence of a vulgarism would be likely to annoy the reader. In the mouth of a Boatswain it can offend no one. We therefore leave it.

NOTE II.

I. 1. 57-59. _Mercy on us!--we split, &c._ It may be doubtful whether the printer of the first folio intended these broken speeches to express “a confused noise within.” Without question such was the author’s meaning. Rowe, however, and later editors, printed them as part of Gonzalo’s speech. Capell was the first editor who gave the correct arrangement.

NOTE III.

I. 2. 173. _princesses._ See Mr Sidney Walker’s _Shakespeare’s Versification_, p. 243 ff. “The plurals of nouns ending in _s_, in certain instances, in _se_, _ss_, _ce_, and sometimes _ge_, ... are found without the usual addition of _s_ or _es_, in pronunciation at least, although in many instances the plural ending is added in printing, where the metre shows that it is not to be pronounced.”

In this and other instances, we have thought it better to trust to the reader’s ear for the rhythm than to introduce a change in spelling that might confuse the sense. The form “princesses,” whose use in Shakespeare’s time was doubted by one of our correspondents, is found in the _History of King Leir_.

Rowe’s reading “princes” might be defended on the ground that the sentiment is general, and applies to royal children of both sexes; or that Sir Philip Sidney, in the first book of the _Arcadia_, calls Pamela and Philoclea “princes.”

NOTE IV.

I. 2. 298. The metre of this line, as well as of lines 301 and 302, is defective, but since no correction can be regarded as completely satisfactory, we have, as is our custom, left the lines as they are printed in the Folio. The defect in the metre of line 298 has only been noticed by Hanmer, who makes the line read:

“Do so, and after two days I’ll discharge thee.”

Possibly it should be printed like this:

“Do so; and

After two days

I will discharge thee.”

There is a broken line, also of four syllables, at line 253 of the same scene, and another of seven at line 235.

There is no reason to doubt that the _words_ are as Shakespeare wrote them, for although the action of the play ends in less than four hours (I. 2. 240 and V. 1. 186), Ariel’s service is not to end until the voyage to Naples is over. Prospero, too, repeats his promise and shows his satisfaction by further shortening the time of servitude: “within two days,” I. 2. 420. Possibly “Invisible” (301) should have a line to itself. Words that occupy a broken line in this way gain a marked emphasis.

But the truth is that in dialogue Shakespeare’s language passes so quickly from verse to prose and from prose to verse, sometimes even hovering, as it were, over the boundary—being rhythmical rather than metrical—that all attempts to make the metre regular must be made with caution and received with doubt.

NOTE V.

I. 2. 377, 378:

_Courtsied when you have and kiss’d_

_The wild waves whist._

This punctuation seems to be supported by what Ferdinand says (391, 392):

“The music crept by me upon the waters,

Allaying both their fury and my passion, &c.”

At the end of the stanza we have printed _Hark, hark! ... The watch-dogs bark_ as that part of the refrain which “sweet sprites bear.” The other part is borne by distant watch-dogs.

NOTE VI.

I. 2. 443. _I fear you have done yourself some wrong._ See this phrase used in a similar sense in _Measure for Measure_, I. 11. 39.

NOTE VII.

II. 1. 27. _Which, of he or Adrian._ “Of” is found in the same construction in _Midsummer Night’s Dream_, III. 2. 336.

‘Now follow if thou darest to try whose right, Of thine or mine, is most in Helena.’

NOTE VIII.

II. 1. 157. _Of its own kind._ There is no doubt, as Dr Guest has shown, that ‘it,’ which is the reading of the 1st and 2nd folios, was commonly used as a genitive in Shakespeare’s time, as it still is in some provincial dialects. ‘Its,’ however, was coming into use. One instance occurs in this play, I. 11. 95, ‘in its contrary.’

NOTE IX.

II. 1. 241. _she that from whom._ Mr Spedding writes: ‘The received emendation is not satisfactory to me. I would rather read, “She that—From whom? All were sea-swallowed &c., i.e. from whom should she have note? The report from Naples will be that all were drowned. We shall be the only survivors.” The break in the construction seems to me characteristic of the speaker. But you must read the whole speech to feel the effect.’

NOTE X.

II. 1. 249-251. All editors except Mr Staunton have printed in italics (or between inverted commas) only as far as ‘_Naples?_’, but as ‘_keep_’ is printed with a small k in the folios, they seem to sanction the arrangement given in our text.

NOTE XI.

II. 1. 267. _Ay, sir; where lies that? if ’twere a kibe._ Mr Singer and Mr Dyce have changed ‘’twere’ to ‘it were’ for the sake of the metre. But then the first part of the line must be read with a wrong emphasis. The proper emphasis clearly falls on the first, third, and fifth syllables, ‘Aý, sir; whére lies thát?’ See Preface.

NOTE XII.

II. 2. 165. Before ‘here; bear my bottle’ Capell inserts a stage direction [_To Cal._], but it appears from III. 2. 62, that Trinculo was entrusted with the office of bottle-bearer.

NOTE XIII.

III. 1. 15. _Most busy lest, when I do it._ As none of the proposed emendations can be regarded as certain, we have left the reading of F1, though it is manifestly corrupt. The spelling ‘doe’ makes Mr Spedding’s conjecture ‘idlest’ for ‘I doe it’ more probable.

NOTE XIV.

III. 3. 17. The stage direction, which we have divided into two parts, is placed all at once in the folios after ‘as when they are fresh’ [Solemne and strange Musicke; and Prosper on the top (invisible:) Enter ... depart].

Pope transferred it to follow Sebastian’s words, ‘I say, to night: no more.’

NOTE XV.

III. 3. 48. _Each putter out of five for one._ See Beaumont and Fletcher, _The Noble Gentleman_, I. 1. (Vol. II. p. 261, ed. Moxon): ‘The return will give you five for one.’ MARINE is about to travel.

NOTE XVI.

IV. 1. 146. _You do look, my son, in a moved sort._ Seymour suggests a transposition: ‘you do, my son, look in a moved sort.’ This line however can scarcely have come from Shakespeare’s pen. Perhaps the writer who composed the Masque was allowed to join it, as best he might, to Shakespeare’s words, which re-commence at ‘Our revels now are ended,’ &c.

NOTE XVII.

IV. 1. 230. _Let’s alone._ See Staunton’s “Shakespeare,” Vol. I. p. 81, note (b).

NOTE XVIII.

V. 1. 309. _Of these our dear-beloved solemnized._ The Folios have ‘belov’d’; a mode of spelling, which in this case is convenient as indicating the probable rhythm of the verse. We have written ‘beloved,’ in accordance with the general rule mentioned in the Preface.

‘Solemnized’ occurs in four other verse passages of Shakespeare. It is three times to be accented ‘sólemnized’ and once (_Love’s Labour’s Lost_, II. 1. 41) ‘solémnized.’
